## Question / scope

For the 42 countries plus EU27 and euro-area (EA) aggregates:
1. Free sources for X01 (nominal GDP, local currency, quarterly and annual), X02 (nominal GDP, USD) and X03 (imports of goods AND services, monthly, USD). For every quarterly GDP source: is the value a **period level** (the quarter's own GDP) or a **seasonally adjusted annual rate (SAAR)** (design P18, risk R36)?
2. A monthly foreign-exchange-reserves route for M14 that does not need automated IMF access.
3. Low priority: can central-bank inflation targets (for M06/M07 `target_distance`, forecasting-amendment R7) be obtained from a public machine-readable source?

Out of scope: architecture, schemas, code, how to convert currencies, how to rank.

## Findings (each with citation: URL, access date)

### F1. Quarterly nominal GDP: period level or SAAR (design P18)

Result in one line: **Eurostat, OECD QNA and the World Bank GEM all publish a quarterly period level (not annualised). The SAAR trap sits in national sources (US BEA, Mexico INEGI, Argentina INDEC) and in anything taken from FRED/Haver-type mirrors of those.**

Evidence for "period level":
- Eurostat `namq_10_gdp`, B1GQ, Germany, `CP_MNAC`, `s_adj=SCA`: 2025-Q1 1,121,547; 2025-Q2 1,129,281; 2025-Q3 1,137,192; 2025-Q4 1,149,224; 2026-Q1 1,159,664; 2026-Q2 1,171,696 million EUR; unit label "Current prices, million units of national currency"; all flagged provisional; `updated` 2026-10-22 [VERIFIED-API 24 Oct, https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/namq_10_gdp?na_item=B1GQ&unit=CP_MNAC&s_adj=SCA&geo=DE]. The unit label does not say "annualised" and the page does not state "not annualised" explicitly. [INFERENCE] Four such quarters sum to about 4.6 trillion EUR, about the size of German annual GDP, so these are quarterly levels.
- OECD QNA, 2025-Q4, calendar and seasonally adjusted, current prices (`ADJUSTMENT=Y`, `PRICE_BASE=V`, `TRANSFORMATION=N`), unit national currency, millions: Germany 1,149,224 (identical to Eurostat), United States 7,865,630.8, Japan 168,750,225, United Kingdom 769,751, Euro area 4,056,051.6, EU 4,799,986.5 [VERIFIED-API 24 Oct, `https://sdmx.oecd.org/public/rest/data/OECD.SDD.NAD,DSD_NAMAIN1@DF_QNA_EXPENDITURE_NATIO_CURR,/Q.Y..S1.S1.B1GQ.....V.N.?startPeriod=2025-Q4&endPeriod=2025-Q4`; I did not read the OECD metadata text that would state "not annualised"]. [INFERENCE] US annual GDP is about 30-31 trillion USD, so 7.87 trillion is a quarter, not an annualised rate. The OECD file also contained NSA rows (`ADJUSTMENT=N`, for example US 2025-Q1 7,380,790) and several other numbers for the same period (different vintages or tables); a query must pin adjustment, price base, transformation and table.
- World Bank GEM `NYGDPMKTPSACN` (GDP, current LCU, millions, seasonally adjusted): US 2025Q1 7,510,500 ... 2026Q2 8,121,500; Germany 2026Q2 1,171,700; Japan 2026Q2 171,929,600; China 2026Q2 36,864,812.58 million CNY (the last four quarters sum to about 144 trillion, consistent with annual GDP of about 140 trillion) [VERIFIED-API 24 Oct, https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/USA;DEU;JPN;BRA/indicator/NYGDPMKTPSACN?source=15&date=2025Q1:2026Q4]. Germany matches Eurostat to rounding (1,171,700 vs 1,171,696). The API has blank unit fields and no "annualised" statement, so this is again an [INFERENCE] from magnitudes.

Evidence for "SAAR" in national sources (the traps):
- US BEA: nominal GDP is published "in billions of dollars at a seasonally adjusted annual rate" (FRED series GDP, source BEA) [SEARCH 24 Oct, FRED series page snippet https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/GDP; the snippet showed Q3 2024 = 29,354.321 billion]. This is about four times the OECD US level above.
- Mexico (INEGI): nominal GDP "seasonally adjusted at annual rate", 2026 Q1 = 36,035,050 million pesos [THIRD-PARTY, Moody's economy.com page https://www.economy.com/mexico/nominal-gross-domestic-product/seasonally-adjusted-at-annual-rate]. OECD's Mexico 2025-Q4 level is 8,936,780.4 (file above), and 36.0 / 8.9 is about 4: consistent with the SAAR reading [INFERENCE].
- Argentina (INDEC): the economy.com page lists nominal GDP as "Not Seasonally Adjusted at Annual Rate", 2026 Q1 = 973,875,781,700 (units not shown) [THIRD-PARTY https://www.economy.com/argentina/nominal-gross-domestic-product]. OECD's 2025-Q4 level for Argentina is 236,375,414.6 million; four quarters of that scale are about 0.95 trillion, so INDEC's annualised figure and the OECD level are consistent in order of magnitude [INFERENCE]. Whether INDEC's own file says "annualised" was not verified.
- Canada: Statistics Canada table 36-10-0104-01 is the current-price quarterly expenditure-based GDP table; whether it is SAAR was **not confirmed** (search returned only release titles) [SEARCH https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260227/dq260227a-cansim-eng.htm]. From memory Statistics Canada reports SAAR [UNVERIFIED].
- Not examined: Japan Cabinet Office, Korea, South Africa (Stats SA/SARB), India, Brazil, Australia, UK, China, Türkiye, Indonesia, Saudi Arabia national releases. Any of them may publish SAAR [UNVERIFIED].

Consequence for the design question (P18): `flow_basis = PERIOD_FLOW` is justified for Eurostat `namq_10_gdp`, OECD QNA and GEM on the evidence above; any national or FRED-mirrored GDP series must be checked per series, and the level-vs-annualised test can be made mechanically: compare the quarterly value against the annual World Bank value (`NY.GDP.MKTP.CN`) for the same calendar year; a ratio near 4 means SAAR. I make that comparison only as a data-quality observation, not as a design.

4. Cadence and effort: targets change rarely; a manually curated table seeded from these two datasets and verified against central-bank statements is the lightest route that would give auditable provenance. This is a statement about effort, not a design proposal.

### F7. Cross-cutting licence and terms observations
- GEM (`source=15`) is the broadest free route found for X01 (quarterly), X02 (quarterly), X03 (goods part) and M14, and it covers China and Saudi Arabia where OECD and Eurostat do not. Its series note names Datastream as the origin. Datastream is a commercial vendor database; the World Bank's own terms page for GEM was **not** retrieved. The only licence statement I found is a Red Hat marketplace listing (CC BY 4.0) [THIRD-PARTY https://marketplace.redhat.com/en-us/products/global-economic-monitor]. Under the approved data-sources note the World Bank's general terms allow non-commercial use (F2), and the project is non-commercial, but the design decision 14b Q4 that "licence is not important for now (diploma project)" should be a conscious choice for a source whose upstream is a vendor.
- A DataBank listing says GEM monthly data are "populated upon availability" and the DataBank homepage text read "GEM was updated on March 31, 2026" [SEARCH 24 Oct]; the API record says `lastupdated` 2026-09-08. The update schedule is therefore unclear and the GEM series for euro-area members stopped in 2026M04.
- Eurostat monthly BoP confidentiality flags (5 countries) are an observed fact, not a policy I could read.

## Risks / unknowns
1. **Fetcher limitation**: no shell; all live results come through a summarising fetcher. One cross-check (OECD file search) agreed with the summaries, and three-way agreement for Germany (Eurostat, OECD, GEM) is reassuring, but counts such as "31 of 42" could contain slips.
2. **ECB unavailable today**: HTTP 503 on the RA6 and information pages; the euro-area reserves route is unverified.
3. **GEM provenance and licence** (Datastream) and unclear update schedule; euro-area reserves stalled at 2026M04.
4. **X03 services** remain unsolved for most of the 15 non-EU countries and for five EU countries (confidential in Eurostat).
5. **SAAR contamination**: three of the sources I examined (US, Mexico, Argentina national series) are annualised or flagged annualised; the others are unexamined. Mixed-source ratios fail by a factor of about four if the flow basis is wrong.
6. **Currency basis at euro adoption** (Bulgaria 2026-01-01, Croatia 2023-01-01): World Bank restates history in euros; other sources may not. A ratio of a leva-valued numerator to a euro-valued denominator would be wrong by a factor of about 1.96 (Bulgaria) [INFERENCE; 1.95583 is the official leva/euro rate, [UNVERIFIED] here].
7. **OECD**: the 60 vs 20 downloads-per-hour conflict remains; I made 6 OECD data calls today (QNA x3, structure x2, KEI x1).
8. **Coverage snapshots are single-period**: a country missing in a 2025-Q4 OECD snapshot may still have older history in that dataflow; start dates were not tested for X01/X02/X03.
9. Request log (all HTTP GET through the fetcher, one at a time): Eurostat 6 (3 `namq_10_gdp`/`bop_c6_m` probes plus 3 filtered `bop_c6_m` calls, one HTTP 413); OECD 6; World Bank 16 (WDI and GEM); BCB 2; ECB 3 (all HTTP 503); FRED 1 page; WTO portal 1; DBnomics 1; Mendeley 1; BIS page 1; ScienceDirect and SSRN pages HTTP 403. No attempt was made to get around any block.

## Open questions for design

For the user (decisions that are not mine):
1. GEM as a source: accept the World Bank GEM (source 15) for X01/X02 quarterly, X03 goods and M14 given the Datastream upstream and the unconfirmed licence (F7)? If not, China and Saudi Arabia have no quarterly GDP route in this note.
2. X01 quarterly for China, Saudi Arabia, Cyprus, Malta: is GEM (all four) or Eurostat (Cyprus, Malta) acceptable as the single source, or must one source cover all 42?
3. X03: goods-only monthly USD exists for about 36-41 of 42, goods-and-services monthly only for the US and, in euro and with five confidential countries, for the EU. Should X03 be reported as `goods_only` for countries without a services route, left n/a, or filled by a documented annual-services bridge (World Bank 2024 is complete)? Should the WTO key be requested to test its monthly services coverage?
4. M14: is a one-to-three-month lag (GEM) acceptable, and should the nine stalled countries (euro-area members and Croatia) wait for the ECB RA6 test, or be shown as n/a, or be refreshed from national central banks despite the 14b Q5 decision?
5. Inflation targets: seed a small curated table (BIS 26-bank dataset plus Zhang plus central-bank statements) now, or defer until the Mendeley file layout and country list are inspected by a human download?

For the design phase (questions only):
6. How to record `flow_basis` for GEM and OECD series whose "not annualised" status rests on magnitude checks rather than on a metadata statement (P18), and whether a mechanical annual-sum check should be a validation rule.
7. Which source is authoritative for quarterly nominal GDP when Eurostat, OECD and GEM agree to rounding (Germany) and for China, Saudi Arabia where only GEM answers.
8. How to handle series with a currency redenomination (Bulgaria, Croatia): restated history (World Bank) vs national-currency history (others), under the "matching currency" alignment rule.
9. How to represent monthly values that are confidential at the source (Ireland, Spain, Croatia, Cyprus, Netherlands in Eurostat BoP) so they do not look like ingestion failures.
10. How to treat GEM's stalled euro-area reserves (last value 2026M04, API still returns nulls up to 2026M08) in the stale-on-arrival and staleness logic.
